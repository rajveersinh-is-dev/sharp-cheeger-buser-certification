# Honest acceptance assessment

Prepared 2026-10-18. Author: Rajveersinh Vishal Pardeshi.
Target journal: **The Quarterly Journal of Mathematics (OUP)**, standard-licence
route.

This assessment describes an **unsubmitted manuscript**. No submission has been
made, no editor or referee has seen it, and nothing below implies external
peer review.

---

## 1. A numerical probability cannot be responsibly estimated

**A defensible numerical probability of acceptance cannot be estimated from the
available evidence.**

Specifically, no OUP-published, QJM-specific acceptance rate was retrieved or

What follows is a **qualitative** assessment with explicit reasoning.

## 2. Desk-rejection risk — HIGH

Reasons:

1. **The core result is classical.** The abstract states this, but a handling
   editor reading only the title and abstract may reasonably conclude that the
   submission re-packages textbook material (Chung, *Spectral Graph Theory*) plus
   a computational check. That is the single strongest reason for rejection at
   the editorial stage.
2. **Subject fit.** QJM is listed by OUP under *Pure Mathematics*. Spectral graph
   theory sits at the boundary between pure mathematics and applied analysis; it
   is publishable there, but the fit is not obvious at first glance and a
   sceptical editor may prefer a more canonical pure-mathematics submission.
3. **Presentation risk.** The manuscript's unusual and honest selling point —
   a documented post-mortem of three bugs in the authors' own code — is
   unfamiliar territory for a mathematics journal. Editors may read it as a
   methodological essay rather than a research contribution.
4. **No external validation.** The work has not been presented, refereed, or
   discussed with specialists. There is no indication that the community finds
   this formulation useful.

**Qualitative category: high risk of desk rejection.**

## 3. Progressing to external review — UNCERTAIN, and conditional

If it survives the editorial screen, it would plausibly reach referees, because
the mathematics is self-contained and correct, the claims are modest, and
nothing in the paper is overstated. Referees would find it easy to verify.

However:

* Referees in pure mathematics typically value *novelty*. A paper whose own
  novelty assessment is "incremental" is a poor bet on referee enthusiasm.
* The most likely referee reaction is "correct, but why publish?" — which is a
  revision request rather than a rejection, but could still sink the paper at a
  second reading.
* The verification pipeline is a genuine asset, but only if referees value
  computer-assisted verification; many pure-mathematics referees will treat it as
  supplementary.

**Qualitative category: uncertain; would likely reach review but with weak
prospects of support.**

## 4. Eventual acceptance after revision — LOW

Even assuming review, the dominant failure mode is priority/novelty, not
correctness. Correctness is the paper's strongest dimension and is not the
bottleneck.

**Qualitative category: low.**

## 6. Most likely reasons for rejection, ranked

1. **"Classical result, insufficient novelty."** Overwhelmingly the most likely.
2. **"Correct but not publishable."** The characterisation is real but thin.
3. **Scope.** A pure-mathematics journal may prefer the work be aimed at a
   computational-mathematics venue.
4. **Presentation.** The post-mortem material could be judged to crowd out the
   mathematics.

## 7. Changes most likely to improve prospects

1. **Sharpen the mathematical contribution.** The single highest-value change
   would be to find a genuinely new result — e.g. determine the exact optimal
   constant for the *loop-aware* Cheeger constant, or the sharp two-sided bound
   in a setting where the classical pair is known to fail. Anything else is
   secondary.
2. **Split the manuscript.** Send the characterisation+verification to a
   computational mathematics or software-oriented venue, and keep only the
   mathematics for QJM.
3. **Reframe.** Lead with the verification methodology and the general recipe
   ("how to certify an inequality of this class"), and submit to a journal with
   a computer-assisted-verification or reproducibility culture.
4. **Remove the retracted claim from prominence.** Keep the honesty, but a
   reviewer skimming will read "found a counterexample to a classical theorem"
   in the abstract area and react badly. State the retraction once, precisely,
   and let the verified results lead.
5. **Widen the exact verification** to `n = 7` unweighted. Improves credibility,
   though it cannot cure the novelty problem.

## 8. Standing caveat

Even a well-executed, technically flawless paper can be rejected for scope,
priority, editorial judgement, or reviewer disagreement. The scores above are my
own assessment of this manuscript, not a forecast of any journal's behaviour.
